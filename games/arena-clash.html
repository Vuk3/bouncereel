Arena Clash

Flags in one round arena, each tied to the ring by a fan of strings. Every bounce off the ring ties ten more, a touch cuts a rival's, and a flag with no strings left is out. The last flag standing wins, in a battle worked out before it plays and balanced to end on your length.

How it plays

The format as its channels play it: nothing appears or goes that a viewer cannot see happen.

  1. Every bounce ties tenOff the ring, where the ball hit it, ten new strings in its colour, however many it already holds.
  2. A touch cutsA ball that touches a rival's strings cuts every one it touches, at once. Its own it passes through.
  3. No strings, outA flag whose last string is cut is out, crossed off in the title and in the row of flags under the ring.
  4. Faster and fasterThe balls speed up as the battle goes on, so it never drags, whatever the length.
  5. Slow motion at the endThe last outs and the win play slowed down; a flag down to its last strings counts in red, with a heartbeat.
  6. Last flag standingTwo flags or twelve, the battle ends when one is left, on the second you picked.

What you choose

Everything on one card at the top of the studio. Leave any of it to the dice.

  • Any of 199 flags

    Every country, found as you type a few letters: srb finds Serbia, usa the United States.

  • Or a region from the dice

    Europe, the Balkans, the Americas, Asia, Africa or Oceania, or the whole world. A battle of one region has its name over the flags.

  • From 2 flags to 12

    Made for three or four; a duel at two, a crowd at twelve. Every one of them balanced to the length.

  • The winner

    On Champion, pick the flag that survives; otherwise the arena decides. Four battles in five end on a comeback.

  • The length

    From 20 to 90 seconds: the balls' starting speed is set so the last flag stands on the second you picked.

  • The strings' music

    Each bounce plays the melody, cut strings are plucked up a harp, a flag out shatters. A narrator says the rule and calls the winner (Champion).

Questions

About Arena Clash. The rest are on the home page.

Is the result random?

It is worked out before it plays, from a seed, and balanced: the studio sets the balls' speed so the last flag stands on your length, and tries seeds until the outs are spread through it. The same settings always play the same battle.

Can I choose who wins?

On Champion, yes: pick the winning flag and the studio finds a battle that flag wins, usually from behind. On the other plans, or left open, any flag can.

Why do strings only come from bounces?

Because a viewer can see it happen: every new string is a bounce they just watched, every lost one a cut. Nothing appears or vanishes on its own, as the format's channels play it.

How many flags can a battle have?

Two to twelve. Three or four reads best in a short: each fan big enough to follow, and an out every few seconds.
