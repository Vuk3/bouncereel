How it plays

Three rules, and the numbers do the rest: a viewer picks a side in the first second and stays for the last digit.

  1. Every floor hit deals a numberEach ball hits with the next number of its sequence: 1, 1, 2, 3, 5… against 1, 2, 6, 24, 120…
  2. Break every layerA layer breaks once its number has been dealt, and the first ball through its last one reaches the line.
  3. Close to the very endSpeeds are set so it ends close, a photo finish or a gap of 1 to 10 s that you pick, at the length you pick.
  4. A power, if you likeA ball can speed up or slow down with every floor hit, grow or split into more balls.
  5. A crowd against oneUp to 200 balls of one sequence against one ball of another, racing as one.
  6. Every digitNumbers are whole, with a separator, never scientific notation: the last digits are what the comments check.

Questions

About Ball Race. The rest are on the home page.

How close are the races?

As close as you ask. A photo finish puts the loser 0.15 to 1.1 seconds behind the winner; or pick a gap of 1 to 10 seconds. Either way the speeds are set so the video comes out at the length you chose.

Which ball wins?

Whichever the race says: the studio finds speeds that end it close, and the sequences decide. Roll again for another.

Are the numbers real?

Every one: the next term of the sequence, to every digit, with a separator. Layers stay at or under 1018 so every digit fits.

Is it on the free plan?

Yes: Ball Race is made on every plan, up to 30 seconds in the paper look on the free one.
